# AGI Briefing — October 31, 2026

**Executive Summary:** AI systems claimed to solve a 27-year-old theoretical computer science problem this week, while major labs race to deploy reasoning-capable frontier models and new safety frameworks take shape in Washington — marking one of the most consequential weeks in the long march toward AGI.

## 1. AI Cracks a 27-Year-Old Pillar of Computer Science

**Source:** Science Magazine | October 28, 2026

OpenAI and Anthropic each claimed AI-generated solutions to multiple new problems in math, computer science, and physics — including an apparent counterexample to the exponential time hypothesis, a long-standing conjecture in computational complexity. An independent theoretical computer scientist called it "a major breakthrough." The work raises new questions about how AI-generated scientific contributions should be attributed and shared.

**Why it matters for AGI:** Solving open mathematical conjectures has long been considered a gold-standard test of machine reasoning. This marks a qualitative shift from AI as a research assistant to AI as a co-discoverer — a key milestone on the path to systems that can advance science autonomously.

**Link:** https://www.science.org/content/article/ai-cracks-27-year-old-pillar-computer-science-openai-and-anthropic-claim

## 2. OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra Framed as "Early-Stage AGI"

**Source:** Bloomberg | September 4, 2026

OpenAI began rolling out GPT-6 Astra in early September, with co-founder Greg Brockman calling it "the start of an AGI era." Bloomberg reports the model represents a significant capability jump, though independent researchers noted ARC-AGI-2 benchmark scores for current frontier systems remain around 4% versus near-100% for humans, highlighting how far definitions of AGI diverge.

**Why it matters for AGI:** The framing from inside a leading AI lab of a commercial product as "early-stage AGI" signals a shift in how these organizations communicate publicly about the timeline. Whether or not the claim holds up, it shapes policy, investment, and public expectation.

**Link:** https://www.bloomberg.com/news/features/2026-09-04/what-is-agi-openai-anthropic-race-for-artificial-general-intelligence

## 3. Inference-Time Scaling Becomes the New Baseline

**Source:** Multiple research reviews | 2026

The widespread adoption of inference-time scaling — giving models more compute at test time to "think" before answering — has become the single biggest architectural shift of 2026. Models like DeepSeek-R1 and OpenAI's o-series demonstrated that reasoning benchmark scores improve substantially without adding base parameters, simply by spending more compute on each query.

**Why it matters for AGI:** This paradigm decouples capability from training cost. It means frontier reasoning ability can be unlocked on demand, enabling systems to apply more deliberate, multi-step reasoning to hard problems — a key property AGI researchers have long sought.

**Link:** https://hakia.com/tech-insights/nlp-2025-review/

## 8. OpenAI and Google Jointly Submit Frontier Model Auditing Framework

**Source:** AI Governance & Risk Brief, AI Conference London | Early October 2026

In an unusual show of collaboration, OpenAI and Google jointly submitted the "Frontier Model Auditing Framework" (FMAF) to standards bodies including NIST (US) and BSI (UK). The framework aims to establish common audit criteria for the most capable AI systems.

**Why it matters for AGI:** Standardized auditing frameworks are a prerequisite for governance at the AGI frontier. Joint industry action on safety standards, if adopted widely, could help ensure that the most capable systems are evaluated consistently before deployment.
