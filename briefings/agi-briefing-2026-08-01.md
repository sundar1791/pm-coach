## 1. AI Disproves the Jacobian Conjecture (Open Since 1939)

**Source:** Skycrumbs Blog / arXiv | **Date:** Week of July 28–August 1, 2026

**Why it matters for AGI:** Mathematician Levent Alpöge used Claude Fable 5 to discover an explicit counterexample to the Jacobian Conjecture, a problem open since 1939. The result was immediately formalized in Lean and submitted to Google DeepMind's Formal Conjectures repo. This establishes a new human-AI collaborative workflow for exploring enormous proof and counterexample spaces — a capability previously thought to require human-level mathematical intuition, making it a landmark moment in AI-assisted science.

**Link:** https://skycrumbs.com/blog/ai-research-july-2026

## 7. "Selective Activation Sparsity" — Models 3x Smaller, Same Reasoning Performance

**Source:** Skycrumbs Blog | **Date:** Week of July 28–August 1, 2026

**Why it matters for AGI:** A new training method called "selective activation sparsity" trains models to activate only the parameters relevant to each specific task. Models trained this way perform comparably to models three times their size on reasoning benchmarks. If this scales, it could collapse the compute requirements for frontier reasoning — enabling AGI-class cognition in edge devices and dramatically accelerating the deployment timeline.

**Link:** https://skycrumbs.com/blog/ai-research-july-2026
