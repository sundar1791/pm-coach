# AGI Progress Briefing — 2026-10-30

**Executive Summary:** OpenAI's claimed AI-generated refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis — one of theoretical computer science's central conjectures — is the week's most consequential development, potentially signaling AI systems operating at the frontier of mathematical research.

## 1. OpenAI Claims AI Refutation of the Exponential Time Hypothesis

**Source:** NewSpaceEconomy.ca analysis, October 29, 2026
**Date:** Announced October 26, 2026 (natural-language disproof produced October 16; formalized in Lean, October 17)

**Why it matters for AGI:** The Exponential Time Hypothesis (ETH) is a foundational conjecture in computational complexity — that 3-SAT cannot be solved in sub-exponential time. An AI system generating a novel, formally verified disproof would represent machine performance surpassing top human mathematicians on original hard problems, a key AGI threshold. The result is still unverified by the broader community, but the speed from conjecture to Lean formalization (one day) is remarkable.

**Link:** [NewSpaceEconomy.ca — OpenAI ETH claim](https://newspaceeconomy.ca/2026/10/29/the-exponential-time-hypothesis-and-openais-claimed-breakthrough-in-computing/)

## 8. Inference-Time Scaling via RLVR Becomes Standard Practice

**Source:** Medium (LLM Reasoning Explosion), ACL 2026 tutorial, alphaxiv.org, 2026
**Date:** Ongoing through 2026

**Why it matters for AGI:** Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR) — training models on tasks with automatically checkable answers — has shifted from research novelty to baseline frontier model feature. Combined with chain-of-thought and inference-time compute scaling, models can now allocate more "thinking" per problem. This architecture mirrors how humans slow down on hard problems, and it underlies many of the 2026 reasoning benchmark gains.
